WAR Efficiency Analysis

Which teams actually get the most out of their spending?

I analyzed 2025 MLB payroll versus WAR production from players to identify which teams accumulated the most WAR per million dollars spent. By normalizing team spending and WAR across both position players and pitchers, I found that WAR per dollar varied significantly across the league. This metric shows us what actually happened, not necessarily what will happen next year.

Key Finding: WAR per dollar is a descriptive metric, not predictive. It tells us about roster composition and efficiency in a given year, but spending patterns and player performance change constantly.

Hitter Sustainability via Process Metrics

Why process metrics tell a better story than results.

I argued why evaluating hitter sustainability benefits scouts and analytics teams more when using process metrics instead of outcome stats. I analyzed contact quality, swing decisions, and batted ball profiles to project true skill and reduce variance in player evaluation. It's not just about what happened. It's about why it happened and what we can expect going forward.

Why This Matters: Process metrics give you a clearer picture of what a hitter can actually do versus what they just happened to do in a small sample.
